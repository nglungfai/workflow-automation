How entries are selected

Rubric v1.0 · Approved by Lungfai Ng on 2026-10-31

Every entry on the reading list is scored against the criteria below. The scores are there so a reader can see why an entry earned its place and where it is weaker, not to rank articles.

There is no total score, because a number implies precision the judgment does not have. Each entry instead gets a verdict that names the trade-off accepted, and any claim that could not be verified is flagged until it is checked.

Scores are drafted by an AI model from the full text of each article, checked against the source, and reviewed by the author whenever a rating is Weak or Unverified, a flag is raised, or the independent second review is missing or disagrees.

How an entry gets its scores

The criteria

Evidence basis

Does it report observed data or outcomes, or argue from opinion?

Strong
Reports observed data from a described sample or a real deployment, with counts or denominators a reader can check.
Partial
Reports some data, a single anecdote, or a benchmark without its sample or conditions.
Weak
Argument, framework or recommendation with no observed results.

Method transparency

Can you see how the numbers or conclusions were produced?

Strong
The procedure can be repeated: selection rule, steps, parameters or thresholds, and where the data or code lives.
Partial
Core steps are described, but at least one element needed to reproduce is missing (selection rule, threshold, prompt, dataset).
Weak
Conclusions are given with no account of how they were produced.

Stated limitations

Does the source say where it does not apply?

Strong
Names specific ways the result could be wrong or fail to generalize, and acts on them (for example a counter-test or a scoped claim).
Partial
Caveats are generic or incidental, or the method's limits are named but not the findings' limits.
Weak
No limitations are stated.

Actionability

Could a practitioner act on it within a week?

Strong
Names concrete first steps a practitioner can start within a week with tools they likely have.
Partial
The direction is clear but needs significant adaptation, or infrastructure the reader may not have.
Weak
An insight with no path to applying it.

Interest disclosed

Does the author have a stake in what they recommend, and is it disclosed?

Strong
The author has no evident stake, or states it plainly up front.
Partial
A stake exists and is disclosed late or in passing, or is evident from context without an explicit statement.
Weak
A stake exists (the author's own product, the employer's tooling) and is not disclosed.

Durability

Does the idea survive the next model release?

Strong
The main idea is independent of any specific model, tool version or vendor.
Partial
The core idea holds but leans on current model capabilities, a specific tool, or a snapshot that will age.
Weak
Tied to a specific model, version or benchmark number likely to be obsolete within months.

What the ratings mean

Strong, Partial and Weak are defined per criterion above. Two more values exist.

  • Unverified: The source was read but the answer could not be determined from it. Requires a flag saying what to check.
  • n/a: The criterion does not apply. Allowed only where a criterion says so.

How each entry is handled

  • If Interest disclosed is Partial or Weak, the entry must carry a stake statement, shown with the verdict.
  • Every flag must be resolved, with a resolution note, before publication. An Unverified rating requires a flag.
  • An entry needs author review (reviewed_by = author) if any rating is Weak or Unverified, any flag exists, or the second review is missing or disagrees. Otherwise it may publish as auto-scored, subject to the monthly audit.
  • Every quote is at most 25 words and must appear verbatim in the stored snapshot.

Scoring guidance

  • Rate the main idea, not the worked example, for Durability and Actionability.
  • Rate what the source states, not the author's reputation or the venue.
  • Illustrative numbers inside advice are not evidence.
  • Negative findings ('not stated', 'no dataset linked') are provisional until confirmed; flag them.

Changelog

v1.0 (approved by the author, 2026-10-31)

  • Six criteria with anchored Strong / Partial / Weak definitions.
  • "Independence" became "Interest disclosed": every source has an interest, so the criterion asks whether the reader is told.
  • "Error rate reported" was proposed and dropped by the author. What it caught is kept elsewhere: unvalidated AI steps are noted under Method transparency, and unverified accuracy claims are raised as flags on Evidence basis.
  • Six binding rules (R1 to R6). A proposed rule requiring an "included despite" line and a framework/opinion label for Weak evidence or method was dropped by the author; a Weak rating still routes the entry to author review (R3).
  • Pilot: three September 2026 entries scored under v1 before it was approved. They are labeled pilot, and no selection-time reasoning was captured for them.
