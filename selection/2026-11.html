How We Use Claude Code to Run a Marketing Agency (Without a Dev Team)

Lalia (RSL/A) · published Mar 2026, scored in the Nov 2026 list

Verdict. Included as a candid, concrete account of running day-to-day marketing operations with a coding agent, including what it cannot do and what the team would do differently. The trade-off accepted: self-reported timings with no method, from an agency that sells the approach, in exchange for specific steps a reader can copy.

Stake. The author runs an agency that sells web design, content and automation services, and the post argues for the approach it sells. The timings are the agency's own and no method is given.

Scored by Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5, single pass, full text read). Second review: Independent blind pass, Opus, from the snapshot only (draft scores not shown); prompt blind-v1, no disagreements. Reviewed by the author.

  • Evidence basisPartial

    Reports a real deployment with before-and-after figures: 1 day to 2 to 3 hours per blog post, 4 hours to 45 minutes for a five-email sequence, 1 week to 1 day for a website feature, and monthly output from 4 to 12 posts. Missing: how the times were measured, over what period or how many items, and how the baseline was set. The 'under $200 a month' total in the takeaways is not itemized beyond the $20 plan. The post is also internally loose: the takeaways say under $200 a month while the bottom line gives $20 a month, and it claims zero custom API code while describing a script that calls an image API.

    “in 2025, 4 blog posts, 2 client site projects, 10 automations. In 2026, 12 blog posts, 5 client site projects, 30 automations.”
    ✓ Checked: Checked 2026-11-01 by text search of the snapshot for measured, tracked, timed, stopwatch, sample, baseline and study: no match; 'average' and 'results' do not describe how the timings were taken. The figures are asserted as 'real numbers from our workflows'.✓ Checked: Checked 2026-11-01 in the browser (alt text read, and the productivity chart viewed). The chart repeats the text figures in hours: blog 8 hours to 2.5, email sequence 4 hours to 45 minutes, website feature 40 hours to 8, labeled 3x and 5x faster. The other images are a hub-and-spoke diagram of the nine tools, a learning-curve sketch and illustrations. No additional data, sample or method appears, so no rating changes.
  • Method transparencyPartial

    Core steps are described for the blog pipeline (editorial calendar, brief, 2,000 to 3,500 words, four generated images, SEO checklist, human review in the CMS) and for the stack (nine tool integrations, one agent). Missing for reproducing: the CLAUDE.md contents, the writing guide, the image prompts and the SEO checklist, plus how the timings were measured.

    “Claude Code writes the full blog post at 2,000 to 3,500 words following our blog writing guide.”
  • Stated limitationsPartial

    Has a section on what the tool cannot do (client relationships, strategy, generic default voice, design decisions, bad client requests) and a 2 to 3 week learning curve. These are limits of the tool, not of the findings: nothing bounds the 3x and 5x claims, which come from one two-person agency's own tasks.

    “Claude Code will build whatever you describe, whether it is a good idea or not.”
  • ActionabilityStrong

    Gives concrete first steps with tools a practitioner likely has: write the context file first, start with one workflow, connect tools on day one, and a worked example of a vague versus a specific prompt. Needs only the $20 plan the post names.

    “Build the CLAUDE.md first, before doing any real work.”
  • Interest disclosedPartial

    The author runs an agency that sells these services and the post ends with a case for the same approach at 'small-business prices'. The stake is evident from context; the post never states it as a stake.

    “This is how a two-person agency delivers enterprise-quality work at small-business prices.”
  • DurabilityPartial

    The core pattern, a standing context file plus connected tools and specific specs, does not depend on one vendor. The worked example is tied to Claude Code and a named stack (Sanity, Vercel, GoHighLevel) and to timings from early 2026 that will age.

    “One agent, 9 integrations, zero custom API code.”

2025: The year in LLMs

Simon Willison · published Dec 2025, scored in the Nov 2026 list

Verdict. Included as the broadest single map of the year for engineers. The trade-off: wide coverage and a few reusable ideas in exchange for thin evidence, no method, and a snapshot that will age.

Stake. The author is an independent writer who runs a paid sponsors-only newsletter. He notes free model access from a vendor he praises and discusses that vendor's tools at length; the essay says so only in passing.

Scored by Claude (single pass, full text read). Second review: Independent blind pass, Opus, from the snapshot only (draft scores not shown); prompt blind-v1, no disagreements. Reviewed by the author.

  • Evidence basisPartial

    A retrospective built on the author's own experience plus a few figures: 110 tools built in 2025, an open-weight ranking dated 30 Dec 2025, and a time-horizon chart from METR. Each is reported without its sample or conditions, and most of the essay is first-hand impression. The two charts are images that the page text does not carry.

    “shows I built 110 of these in 2025!”
    ✓ Checked: Opened the page in a browser on 2026-11-01, scrolled to both images and read zoomed screenshots. Open-weight chart (Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, higher is better), left to right: GLM-4.7 68, Kimi K2 Thinking 67, MiMo-V2-Flash 66, DeepSeek V3.2 66, MiniMax-M2.1 64, gpt-oss-120B (high) 61, Qwen3 235B A22B 2507 57, Apriel-v1.6-15B-Thinker 57, gpt-oss-20B (high) 52, DeepSeek R1 0528 52, NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Nano 52, K2-V2 (high) 46, Mistral Large 3 38, QwQ-32B 38 (hatched, marked estimate), NVIDIA Nemotron 9B V2 37, OLMo 3 32B Think 36. It matches the prose: five Chinese models on top, gpt-oss-120B sixth. It adds the scores, the 16-model list, the estimate marking and the single-index basis. METR chart: y-axis is task duration for humans (0 to 5 hours) at a 50% success rate, x-axis is release date 2019 to 2025; read values are approximate, Claude Opus 4.5 about 4h40m, GPT-5.1-Codex-Max about 2h45m, GPT-5 about 2h15m, with error bars that widen sharply for the 2025 models and a dashed exponential trend line. This agrees with the prose (multi-hour tasks in 2025); the chart carries no doubling-time figure, so the seven-month claim comes from METR text outside the image. No contradiction found.
  • Method transparencyWeak

    Conclusions are given with no account of how they were reached: no rule for which trends made the list, no data collection, no prompts or code behind the claims. The author's own experience is the method.

    ✓ Checked: Searched the snapshot with grep (methodolog*, selection rule/criteria, how I chose/picked/selected, criteria, threshold) on 2026-11-01: no hits. The footer links, including Disclosures, were not opened.
  • Stated limitationsPartial

    Caveats are personal and scattered rather than a stated scope: doubts that the METR doubling trend will hold, a port that is not yet fit for untrusted code, and local models not yet trusted for agent work. The conformance-suite finding is tied to ~November 2025 models. There is no section on where the essay's conclusions might be wrong or fail to generalize.

    “I’m not convinced that pattern will continue to hold”
  • ActionabilityPartial

    The strongest transferable steps are to hand a coding agent an existing test suite to work against and to prefer plain command-line tools over extra integrations. They sit inside a year-in-review with no setup or sequence, so a practitioner must extract and adapt them. Most sections report what happened, not what to do.

    “remarkably effective if you can give them an existing test suite to work against”
  • Interest disclosedPartial

    A stake exists and is disclosed late and in passing: free model allowance from a vendor the essay praises, and a paid sponsors-only newsletter promoted at the end. The essay also recommends the vendor's tools at length, and the author's own command-line tool is mentioned. No up-front statement. The footer Disclosures page was not opened.

    “free allowance (from previewing one of their models—thanks, Anthropic)”
    ✓ Checked: Opened the footer Disclosures link (simonwillison.net/about/#disclosures) on 2026-11-01. It states: no compensation for writing about specific topics; weekly sponsorship by text banner and newsletter message since February 2026, with no editorial influence; part of Datasette Cloud work sponsored by Fly.io and GitHub Sponsors support; one day a week working for Prime Radiant; Python Software Foundation board member; unpaid GitHub Star, paid for the 2023 GitHub Accelerator; past Mozilla support in 2023-2024; no payments from LLM vendors, but frequent preview invitations from OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini and Mistral, often under NDA or embargo, often with free API credits and event invitations; one exception where OpenAI paid for time at a GPT-5 preview; occasional EthicalAds and Twitter revenue share; ad-hoc consulting and training, with conflicts to be disclosed in the relevant post. It is a standing site page, not part of the essay.
  • DurabilityPartial

    Several threads outlast any release: agents as models that run tools in a loop, the lethal trifecta, normalization of deviance, and test suites as a guide for agents. But the essay is a dated snapshot of named models, rankings and prices, and its own claims are tied to frontier models of late 2025.

    “the latest coding agents against the ~November 2025 frontier models are remarkably effective”

Don't Break the Cache: An Evaluation of Prompt Caching for Long-Horizon Agentic Tasks

Lumer et al. (PricewaterhouseCoopers) · published Jan 2026 (v2 Jan 31), scored in the Nov 2026 list

Verdict. Included for its checkable measurements and a rule that will outlast the numbers. The trade-off accepted: one benchmark and one kind of agent, January 2026 models and prices, and no code or data linked, in exchange for real sample sizes and a clear design.

Scored by Claude (single pass, full text read). Second review: Independent blind pass, Opus, from the snapshot only (draft scores not shown); prompt blind-v1, 1 disagreement. Reviewed by the author.

  • Evidence basisStrong

    Reports measured cost and time to first token for four models from three providers, four cache conditions each, with n=40 sessions per condition, a named benchmark and t-tests against a no-cache baseline. Tables 1 and 2 give per-model, per-strategy figures a reader can check. One inconsistency: the abstract gives the latency gain as 13-31%, while Table 1 (and the results text) shows 6.1% for one model.

    “For each model, we conduct 40 independent agent sessions per cache condition”
  • Method transparencyPartial

    Core steps are described: models, benchmark, agent library, how cache boundaries were forced with unique identifiers, warmup calls, a 24-hour gap between conditions, cost from reported token counts, and the test used. Missing: where the code or data lives, the 10,000-token system prompt itself, the number of sessions behind each ablation point, and the search tool and run dates.

    “we wait sufficient time (exceeding 24 hours) to ensure cache entries expire based on provider TTL policies”
    ✓ Checked: Re-run 2026-11-01 on the complete live page (article text 48,026 chars, ending where the snapshot ends): the 98 links in the article outside the bibliography are only mailto and in-page anchors, with no external URL; the only github links are 12 reference-list entries for third-party tools. A grep of the snapshot for github, repository, released, available at and http found only reference entries. The arXiv abstract page and PDF were not checked.
  • Stated limitationsPartial

    Caveats are incidental. It says latency numbers vary with server load, uses a 500-token control below the caching threshold, scopes the cost result to a system prompt that dominates the context, and says provider details may change and readers should test their own workloads. Missing: any statement of where the findings may not generalize, such as one benchmark, one web-search agent, a single run window, and no cross-user or cross-session caching.

    “TTFT measurements exhibit natural variance due to factors including server load, network conditions, and provider infrastructure.”
    ✓ Checked: Re-run 2026-11-01 on the complete live article text: no match for limitation, caveat, threat, future work or generaliz. Headings run from 1 Introduction to 7 Conclusion plus appendices A to C, with no limitations heading.
  • ActionabilityStrong

    The main idea is concrete and cheap to test: keep the system prompt static, move timestamps and session identifiers to its end, keep tool definitions fixed within a session, and cache only the stable prefix. A practitioner can check cached-token counts in their own API responses and try it within a week.

    “Common patterns that inadvertently break the cache include timestamps, datetime strings, session identifiers”
  • Interest disclosedStrong

    All seven authors are listed under one consulting firm in the byline, which does not make or sell any of the three providers' caching that is evaluated. No author affiliation with OpenAI, Anthropic or Google is shown. No conflict, funding or disclosure statement appears, and none seems needed. The paper cites the authors' own earlier work on dynamic tool selection, and its advice runs against that approach.

    ✓ Checked: Re-run 2026-11-01 on the complete live article text: no match for funding, acknowledg, disclos, conflict, competing, sponsor, affiliat or partnership (the only hits on the page are the arXiv site footer's generic funder line, outside the paper). The byline names one employer and no provider. Not checked outside the paper, such as the firm's commercial relationships.
  • DurabilityPartial

    The core rule, keep the prompt prefix stable and put changing content last, follows from how prefix caching works. But the paper's numbers and strategy ranking belong to four named models, January 2026 prices and thresholds, and each provider's current caching behavior (one model got slower with full-context caching). The paper itself says these details will change.

    “These implementation details are subject to change and practitioners should consult current provider documentation”

Large Behavior Model: A Promptable Digital Twin of the Retail Customer

Modecrua et al. (Amity AI Holdings) · published Jul 2026 (v2 Jul 10), scored in the Nov 2026 list

Verdict. Included as a close published parallel to person-plus-context behavioral modeling, with a testable claim: evidence in the prompt matters more than model size. The trade-off accepted: one unnamed retailer, 1,500 customers, single point accuracies, one prompted baseline and no code or data linked, in exchange for a detailed recipe and results that include its own losses.

Stake. The authors work at an AI company (Amity AI Holdings); the paper pitches self-hosted deployment of the model and has no funding or competing-interest statement. The retailer that supplied the transactions is not named.

Scored by Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5, single pass, full text read). Second review: Independent blind pass, Opus, from the snapshot only (draft scores not shown); prompt blind-v1, 1 disagreement. Reviewed by the author.

  • Evidence basisStrong

    Reports observed results with counts. Table 1 gives the primary cohort (159,667 training and 40,665 test transactions, 1,500 customers, 38,516 products), and results carry denominators: n=5,091 (classic-B), n=2,490 (B-hard), 12,480 voucher cases, 414 survey users. Limits of the evidence: single point accuracies with no interval, one prompted GPT-5.5 as the only in-domain comparison, and figures that do not reconcile directly (Section 4.1.1 gives 88.7% vs 80.1% for a best configuration on a 662-user set, Table 3 gives 82.2% vs 72.7%).

    “consisting of 12,480 held-out cases with no session overlap with training data”
    ✓ Checked: Checked 2026-11-01 by text search of the full snapshot for confidence interval, variance, bootstrap, seed, standard deviation, error bar and runs. The only hit is Section 5 saying reliable evaluation needs variance-aware metrics rather than point accuracy alone; every results table reports a single value. Under the anchor this does not change Evidence (counts and denominators are given), and it is named in the Limitations and verdict notes.
  • Method transparencyPartial

    Core steps and parameters are given: 4-bit Qwen3-8B and Qwen3.5-9B, LoRA rank 8 and alpha 16, top-3 ChromaDB retrieval used in both training and inference, GRPO for 300 to 500 steps with 8 rollouts and hand-written YES/NO and Jaccard rewards, and an LLM judge with a 0.08% judge-error rate. Missing for reproducing: the retailer and data (not released), the Shopping-DNA prompt template, the baseline's prompt and context, the continued pre-training corpus size, how negatives were built beyond intra- and cross-category, and which data the Table 4 row was tuned on (it is labeled SFT-Lazada in the in-domain retailer test). The paper says price-ending leakage is controlled but not how.

    “top-3 SKUs per decision; present in both training and inference”
    ✓ Checked: Checked 2026-11-01. All 21 outbound links in the article body (read from the live HTML in the browser) are reference entries (DOI or arXiv abstract links); the only other URL in the snapshot is a Hugging Face collection cited as reference 25. A text search for github, repository, released, open-source and publicly found nothing relevant. The arXiv abstract page lists comments '17 pages, 5 figures' and no code link; its Code, Data, Media tab holds only third-party finder toggles (alphaXiv, CatalyzeX, DagsHub, Hugging Face and others), all off, and I did not switch any on.✓ Checked: Checked 2026-11-01 by text search of the snapshot for same prompt, identical, prompted, zero-shot and without such evidence. No sentence states the baseline's prompt or context. The Figure 2 caption calls it a zero-shot frontier model, and Section 5 says frontier models 'without such evidence' stay limited, which suggests but does not confirm the baseline lacked the profile. The paper is silent, so this is counted as missing under Method.
  • Stated limitationsStrong

    Names specific ways it fails to generalize and acts on them. The conclusion lists handcrafted text summaries, degradation when signal sits in IDs or engineered features, and no learned user representation. The paper reports its own losses (Shopee 0.65 vs 0.825 for GPT-5.5, Tmall below a feature-engineered baseline, D2 trip discrimination 68.9% vs 81.2%) and scopes review generation as qualitative only. Not acknowledged: one unnamed retailer and 1,500 customers against a claim that it scales to millions of users, no variance, and a single prompted frontier baseline.

    “Performance also degrades when predictive signals are primarily encoded in platform-specific identifiers or highly engineered numerical features”
  • ActionabilityPartial

    The cheap, testable idea is evidence legibility: rewrite a customer's history as a short plain-language profile, add one readable similarity line for candidate items, and compare against a baseline without them. In the paper's difficulty ladder that moved hard-negative accuracy from near chance to 95.9%. The full recipe (continued pre-training, SFT and GRPO, run on 8 H100 GPUs) needs transaction data at scale and training infrastructure, and the profile template is not provided.

    “The bottleneck is prompt signal, not weights.”
  • Interest disclosedPartial

    A commercial stake is evident from context, not stated: the byline lists Amity AI Holdings Co., Ltd., and the paper pitches self-hosted deployment of the model. No funding, competing-interest or data-source statement appears, and the retailer that supplied the transactions is unnamed.

    “Amity AI Holdings Co., Ltd.”
    ✓ Checked: Checked 2026-11-01 by text search of the snapshot for funding, funded, disclos, conflict, competing, sponsor, acknowledg and grant: no match (only 'anonymized' in the data section). The arXiv abstract page shows no funding text. The byline names Amity Research and Application Center and Amity AI Holdings Co., Ltd. with a company email domain. Not checked outside the paper: whether the company sells behavior-simulation products or supplied the retail data.
  • DurabilityStrong

    The main idea, ground the model in an individual's own transaction evidence and keep person and environment separate, does not depend on a model or vendor. The worked example (one retailer, two small open models, and a gap against one prompted GPT-5.5) is a snapshot that will age, and the paper's fifth contribution is framed as beating frontier models.

    “behavioral simulation is primarily an information problem rather than a model-size problem”

Generative AI and Sales Productivity: Field Experiments in Online Retail

Fang, Yuan, Zhang, Donati, Sarvary · arXiv Oct 2025 (v6 Jun 2026), scored in the Nov 2026 list

Verdict. Included for randomized evidence at a scale few practitioner sources offer, a candid limitations section, and a reported failure (ad titles). The trade-off accepted: an unnamed platform with a partner-affiliated co-author, no data, code or model details, two of seven results taken from the platform's own unverified analyses, and a headline chatbot gain measured against no service rather than against human agents.

Stake. One co-author is listed as affiliated with the partner company that ran the experiments, chose the workflows and supplied the data. The paper has no funding or conflict-of-interest statement and says the firm anticipates substantial value from GenAI.

Scored by Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5, single pass, main text read in full, appendices read in the browser). Second review: Independent blind pass, Opus, from the snapshot only (draft scores not shown); prompt blind-v1, no disagreements. Reviewed by the author.

  • Evidence basisStrong

    Reports randomized field experiments with checkable denominators: Table 5 observations range from 44,614 (pre-sale chatbot) to 13,715,528 (marketing push), with covariate balance tables in Appendix B. Limits of the evidence: two of the seven workflows (chargeback defense, live chat translation) rest on the platform's own analyses with no significance reported, and the paper says the translation result could not be independently verified; outcomes in some tables are normalized to control means; the headline 16.3% chatbot gain is against a no-service control, while GenAI against human agents showed no significant difference.

    “with sample sizes ranging from tens of thousands to tens of millions of users or products”
    ✓ Checked: Checked 2026-11-01 in the browser. Appendix A is UI screenshots with captions; Appendix B is the covariate balance table (all but one control-treatment p-values above 0.1; Google Advertising Title past sales p=0.084); Appendix C gives the per-workflow models and the extra chatbot arms (GenAI vs human agents not significantly different; GenAI plus human escalation vs human only +11.5%, p<0.1), robustness to logit, covariates and excluding the under-1% overlap, and the sentence that the translation estimate could not be independently verified; Appendices D to F are heterogeneity tables consistent with Section 4. No statement of funding, data access or code appears. No rating changes; the unverified-translation point is added to the Evidence note.✓ Checked: Checked 2026-11-01 by text search of the full page for pre-regist, preregist, multiple (hypothesis or testing), Bonferroni and false discovery: no relevant match (the word 'multiple' appears only in other senses). Many outcomes and three heterogeneity dimensions are reported at p<0.1, 0.05 and 0.01 without adjustment. Under the Evidence anchor (observed data with counts) this does not change the rating; it is named under Limitations.
  • Method transparencyPartial

    Core steps are given: randomization unit (consumer, product for the ad-title test), durations from nine days to two months, treatment and control definitions, an OLS specification with entry-day (and language) cohort fixed effects and heteroskedasticity-robust errors, balance checks. Missing for reproducing: the platform and the GenAI model are not named or described beyond 'not fine-tuned' for the ad-title test, the randomization procedure is not described and the chatbot arms are unequal (15,457 control and 29,157 treatment consumers in Appendix B) without explanation, how the platform chose workflows is only 'managerial judgment', and no data or code is released.

    “We estimate Equation (1) via OLS, adjusting the standard errors for heteroskedasticity.”
    ✓ Checked: Checked 2026-11-01. The outbound links on the arXiv HTML page are three reference URLs (a venture-capital essay, a newspaper leader, a market-research report) plus arXiv and LaTeXML site chrome; the abstract page's comments field holds only keywords and JEL codes. Text search for github, repository, replication, code availability and data availability found only the 'Data Availability' column of Table 2, which says which experiments had granular data. Counted as missing under Method.✓ Checked: Checked 2026-11-01 by text search of the full page for GPT, LLM, Llama, Qwen, Gemini, Claude, large language and foundation model: only a generic 'foundation models' sentence in the ad-title discussion. The model, its version and its prompts are never given; the platform is described only as a leading cross-border e-commerce platform. Counted as missing under Method and as a Durability caveat.
  • Stated limitationsStrong

    Names specific ways the result could be wrong or not generalize and acts on them. The conclusion lists the short horizon (days to months), management-selected workflows, unmeasured labor and capital inputs, possible intertemporal or cross-category substitution (with a non-tabulated check on other purchases), a single platform and no competitor response. The mechanism section is scoped as suggestive, the chatbot result gets a harder comparison against human agents, and the Google ad-title experiment is reported as a failure. Not acknowledged: the partner-affiliated co-author, no multiple-testing adjustment, and the cost side (no ROI, top-line sales only).

    “the evidence in this section is best interpreted as suggestive evidence on channels, rather than as definitive evidence”
  • ActionabilityPartial

    The direction is usable: the largest gain came where the baseline was no service or scarce human capacity, content generation gave low single digits, per-message personalization needed volume, and a model not tuned for ads lost. A practitioner can run a randomized holdout on one workflow and read conversion before sales. It needs a platform with enough traffic to detect 1 to 3% effects, the effect sizes do not transfer, and no prompts, tuning recipe or model are given.

    “GenAI improves sales performance by performing tasks that are difficult for human agents to fulfill due to talent shortages or resource constraints.”
  • Interest disclosedPartial

    A stake exists and is disclosed only through the author block and context: one co-author is listed under 'Partner Company', the firm that ran the experiments, chose the workflows and supplied the data, and the text says the firm anticipates substantial value from GenAI. The paper has no funding or conflict-of-interest statement and does not say what the partner shaped or reviewed.

    “We partnered with a leading cross-border e-commerce platform to identify and quantify the sales productivity gains from GenAI adoption in online retail.”
    ✓ Checked: Checked 2026-11-01 by text search of the full page (main text and appendices) for funding, acknowledg, thank, conflict, declaration, disclos, IRB and ethic: no match. The only disclosure of the partner relationship is the affiliation line in the author block and the introduction's sentence about partnering. Under the Interest anchor this is Partial (evident from context, no explicit stake statement), not Weak, because the affiliation is printed on the first page.
  • DurabilityPartial

    The core pattern, that gains scale with GenAI's marginal contribution over the baseline and arrive through conversion rather than basket size, does not depend on one model. The effect sizes come from 2023 to 2024 deployments of an unnamed model, will move with model quality, and the authors say the platform had grown to over 60 workflows by 2025.

    “Instead, it appears to arise from differences in the marginal contribution of GenAI relative to baseline conditions.”
