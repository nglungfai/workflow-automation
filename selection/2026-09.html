01

The State Of AI Harness Engineering 2026

François Zaninotto (Marmelab) · Sep 2026

Verdict. Included for the empirical base: it measures how often teams test their agent rules, and it states its own sampling bias. Read the AI-aggregated trends with the validation gap in mind.

Stake. Marmelab builds AI harnesses for customers; its own product, Atomic CRM, is one of the 11 finalists.

Scored by Claude (single pass, full text read). Second review: Independent blind pass, Opus, from the snapshot only (draft scores not shown), 2 disagreements. Reviewed by the author.

  • Evidence basisStrong

    246 repositories plus a 145-project counter-test; 97 harnesses screened, 11 finalists.

    “Out of the 97 harnesses big enough to test, 60% have neither a test nor an eval.”
  • Method transparencyPartial

    The 246-row table and the screening criteria (commits per harness file, authors, age, dormancy) are published inline. The counter-test is described by examples (Symfony, Rails, Django, React, Kubernetes) with no stated rule for what counts as large, the search used to find the 57 publications is not described, no dataset or scripts are linked beyond individual repositories, and the AI-assisted aggregation of sources is not validated.

    “We used AI to gather, rank, and aggregate trends from the numerous sources we've identified.”
    ✓ Checked: Confirmed in a browser on 2026-10-31 after expanding the collapsed 'Our sources' section: it lists the criteria and the full table; no counter-test selection rule, no publication search method, and no dataset or script link (page anchors checked).
  • Stated limitationsStrong

    Names its sampling bias, separates opinion from finding, and says the article itself will age badly.

    “Repositories that call themselves harnesses are a biased sample, so we’ve run a counter-test”
  • ActionabilityStrong

    Replace prose rules with executable checks, then prune controls as models improve.

  • Interest disclosedPartial

    Disclosed in the body, not the header. The author's own product is one of the 11 finalists.

    “We write AI harnesses ourselves, for our customers and open-source projects.”
  • DurabilityPartial

    The practices (test the harness, keep it minimal, enforce rules with scripts, retire stale controls) do not depend on a model. The audit is a dated 2026 snapshot, measured mostly through Claude Code files and hooks, and the authors say it will age.

02

The Anatomy of Harness Engineering: How to Evaluate, Iterate, and Guard AI Coding Agents

Mullen & Gunderman (Google) · Sep 2026

Verdict. Included as a method, not as evidence. Its value is a concrete first step toward scored per-skill tests.

Stake. Published on the Google Developers Blog; the worked example uses the Antigravity SDK. The page names job titles but no employer.

Scored by Claude (single pass, full text read). Second review: Independent blind pass, Opus, from the snapshot only (draft scores not shown), 2 disagreements. Reviewed by the author.

  • Evidence basisWeak

    A prescriptive framework. No measured results; the only figures are illustrative advice.

    “not to celebrate when you make the agent 2% better”
  • Method transparencyWeak

    The article shows how to build behavioral evals but gives no account of how the authors reached their conclusions: no data, selection rule or measurements behind the claims. The linked repo covers the SDK example only.

    ✓ Checked: Confirmed on the full page in a browser, 2026-10-31: LLM-as-a-judge is named once with no prompt, no batch sizes and no pass thresholds. Authors are listed by job title only; no employer is named on the page.
  • Stated limitationsWeak

    Notes that single runs are noisy and that behavioral evals complement end-to-end suites. Neither is a limit of the recommendations themselves: no failure case or scope condition is given, and the reliability of LLM-as-a-judge is not discussed.

    “single eval runs that can be noisy due to nondeterminism of AI models”
  • ActionabilityStrong

    Pick one failure mode, write a strict check, and re-run it whenever the model changes.

  • Interest disclosedPartial

    The Google Developers Blog host and the Google SDK example make the stake evident, but nothing states it. The worked example is the vendor's own SDK, linked once in passing.

    “Example written for the Antigravity SDK. Explore the full repo.”
  • DurabilityStrong

    Per-failure-mode checks hold across models. The worked example is SDK-specific.

03

How we closed 1,500 GitHub issues in one month

Marcos Hernanz (Next.js) · Sep 2026

Verdict. Included as the only entry here with real operational data and a reusable pattern for agent work with consequences. Its accuracy evidence is a reopen rate (0.2% as of Sept 4, inside a 14-day window), not an audit, so read it as a lower bound on errors.

Stake. Written by the Next.js project; built on Vercel's own eve framework and Sandbox.

Scored by Claude (single pass, full text read). Second review: Independent blind pass, Opus, from the snapshot only (draft scores not shown), 2 disagreements. Reviewed by the author.

  • Evidence basisStrong

    Throughput is well documented: the backlog peaked at 3,109 (Jan 2025), stood at 2,244 on Aug 10, 2026, and 1,462 issues were closed in about three weeks while 218 new reports arrived. Accuracy is supported only by a proxy: 3 of 1,462 closed issues (0.2%) were reopened as of Sept 4, 2026. Reopening needs someone to ask within a 14-day window and many closures were still inside it on that date, so the figure is a floor on errors, not an audit.

    “That includes some issues maintainers closed outside this review.”
    ✓ Checked: Chart read in a browser on 2026-10-31: 99.8% remained closed (1,459), 3 reopened, as of September 4, 2026. Recorded in the evidence note with its limits.
  • Method transparencyStrong

    Four-step investigation in a fresh sandbox, structured output (confidence, evidence, references), a read-only agent, a prompt-injection guard and a reopen Action.

  • Stated limitationsPartial

    Admits the earlier inactivity bot closed issues it should have kept. Offers the reopen rate as its only accuracy check; no independent audit of the agent's calls and no failure modes are reported.

    “closed some reports we should have kept”
  • ActionabilityStrong

    Read-only by default, evidence per verdict, a review queue and a cheap undo. The infrastructure is Vercel's own, so the pattern transfers more easily than the code.

  • Interest disclosedPartial

    Built on Vercel's eve and Sandbox and written by the project owner. No explicit disclosure, though the relationship is evident.

  • DurabilityStrong

    The pattern is not model-specific. The article is a moving target: it is already extending to auto-close.
