The practice — using AI tools day to day: prompting, AI-assisted coding, and automating our own workflows.

01

The State Of AI Harness Engineering 2026

François Zaninotto (Marmelab) · Sep 2026Core

An audit of 246 open-source repositories and 57 publications finds that most projects write agent instructions but rarely test or enforce them; the author recommends keeping harnesses minimal, replacing prose rules with executable checks, and pruning controls as models improve.

Apply it: Turn the rules in our skills into checks that run, not paragraphs the agent may ignore — and prune the ones newer models no longer need. In practice: Prompt vs. Context vs. Harness vs. Loop.

02

The Anatomy of Harness Engineering: How to Evaluate, Iterate, and Guard AI Coding Agents

Mullen & Gunderman (Google) · Sep 2026Core

Argues for behavioral evaluations that check small, observable steps — did the agent run a validator before finishing? — alongside end-to-end benchmarks, starting from a single failure mode and tracking batch results over time.

Apply it: A concrete way to start the scored, per-skill test cases our workflows still lack: pick one failure mode, write a strict check for it, and re-run it whenever the model changes. In practice: How This Site Ships Itself.

03

How we closed 1,500 GitHub issues in one month

Marcos Hernanz (Next.js) · Sep 2026Core

The Next.js team built a read-only research agent that scores whether each old issue can be closed, with a conservative confidence score; maintainers reviewed the evidence, a reopen window checked the calls, and 1,462 issues were closed in about three weeks.

Apply it: A reusable pattern for agent work with real consequences: read-only by default, evidence attached to every verdict, a human review queue, and a cheap way to undo mistakes.

Research papers

The science — foundational methods and applied research behind audience modeling, response, and measurement.

04

Pseudo-Incrementality Testing: Measuring Advertising Lift from Naturally Occurring Interventions

Filippou, Quach & Jha · Sep 2026Core

Treats a firm's own abrupt marketing changes — budget cuts, channel launches, paused programs — as quasi-experiments, dating them with Bayesian inference and estimating impact against a structural time-series counterfactual; on Meta's GeoLift benchmarks its ad-removal estimate came within 6.2% of the experimental result.

Apply it: A route to defensible lift estimates when a randomized holdout isn't feasible — useful for partner and client measurement conversations.

05

How Much of a Harness Does a Strong Agent Need for Autonomous ML Engineering?

Brilliantov, Hernández-Cano & Abbé · Sep 2026Core

Under an equal time budget and the same frontier model, leading open-source harnesses showed no advantage over a single session of a minimal coding agent with read, write, and bash tools; ablations suggest the extra orchestration layers are largely redundant.

Apply it: Evidence for starting with the simplest agent setup and adding structure only when a measured failure demands it — before building orchestration around a model workflow.

01

Practical Loop Engineering

Addy Osmani · Aug 2026Core

Osmani shows how he runs many parallel agents with goal-driven and scheduled loops, and sorts loops into agentic, goal-based, time-based, and proactive types, with example prompts.

Apply it: The practical follow-on to loop design: bound each task with a measurable endpoint, schedule what needs to persist, and delegate the task but never the judgment. In practice: Prompt vs. Context vs. Harness vs. Loop.

02

AI agents can't yet do open-ended AI research

Kapoor & Narayanan (AI Snake Oil) · Aug 2026Core

Princeton researchers tested frontier agents with “shadow evaluations” on two unpublished papers; the original authors rejected both outputs, and logs showed weak research judgment, poor budget awareness, and little response to feedback.

Apply it: A calibrated view of where agents are reliable (execution) and where they are not (judgment) — the case for keeping a human gate on anything open-ended. In practice: How This Site Ships Itself.

03

Domain-Driven Design matters more when AI writes your code

Three Dots Labs · Aug 2026Core

Argues that as agents take over implementation, understanding the business problem and agreeing on design and shared vocabulary before any code is written becomes the hard, valuable part.

Apply it: Agree on definitions and naming before pointing an agent at a pipeline or a skill — it is what lets us verify what it produces.

04

Patterns for Building LLM-based Systems & Products

Eugene Yan · 2023Core

Seven practical patterns for putting LLMs into production — evals, retrieval, fine-tuning, caching, guardrails, defensive UX, and user feedback — organized by whether they improve performance or cut cost and risk.

Apply it: A pattern catalog to check any new AI workflow against before it ships: which of the seven do we have, and which are we skipping?

05

Your AI Product Needs Evals

Hamel Husain · 2024Core

Argues that LLM products stall without evaluation tailored to their own users and tasks, and lays out three levels — unit tests, human and model-based review, and A/B tests — using a production assistant as the case study.

Apply it: The blueprint for testing our agent workflows: start with cheap deterministic checks, then add graded review, then live experiments.

06

PinSage: A New Graph Convolutional Neural Network for Web-Scale Recommender Systems

Pinterest · He et al. · 2018Core

Pinterest builds embeddings for billions of items with a graph neural network that samples neighbors via random walks, separating items that look alike but mean different things; it won in offline tests, user studies, and A/B tests.

Apply it: A step beyond sequence-only embeddings for audience vectors — graph context can sharpen similarity where behavior alone is sparse.

Research papers

The science — foundational methods and applied research behind audience modeling, response, and measurement.

07

From Prediction to Incrementality: Causal Optimization for Large-Scale Targeting and Recommendation

Wei et al. · Aug 2026Core

Combines a Transformer-based causal network for individual treatment effects, a Bayesian bandit layer for exploration, and a constrained-allocation layer; an online A/B test on marketing traffic showed a 7.20% lift in its primary long-term-value metric.

Apply it: Moves targeting from “who is likely to convert” to “who is changed by the treatment” — a template for incrementality-aware audience selection under budget constraints.

08

Incremental Recommendation via Causal Models

Vlontzos et al. · Aug 2026Core

Turns an existing production recommender into a causal model using holdback data already being collected, with a dual-threshold policy; in a large A/B test it cut recommendation impressions by 7% without a meaningful drop in consumption.

Apply it: Shows how existing holdout groups can double as causal training data — a cheap route to incrementality signal without new experiments.
